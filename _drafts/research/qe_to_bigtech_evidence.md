# QE / test-automation engineer (10–15 yrs, bank + outsourcing-firm background) → Google / Microsoft / Amazon / Apple / Meta: evidence notes

Research date: 2026-10-13. Method note: the session's WebSearch budget was exhausted before this task started, so everything below came from direct fetches of primary pages, public job-board APIs (Google Careers results HTML, amazon.jobs search.json, Greenhouse/Ashby boards, LinkedIn guest job-count HTML) and a short Chrome session for Google SERPs/LinkedIn. Reddit was blocked by every path available (fetch, pullpush, and the Chrome extension's site policy), Blind sits behind a cookie-consent wall I did not accept, and Glassdoor/Medium returned 403. Anecdote is labelled ANECDOTE; official/employer pages are labelled OFFICIAL; survey/data sources are labelled DATA.

## KQ1. Transition stories: service-company QA/SDET → Google/Microsoft/Amazon

### Takeaway
I found one named, well-documented account (a 10–14-year TCS tester who moved to Google and later became an engineering leader, but in Bengaluru, not North America) and several thread titles that confirm the path exists, but no fully-read, North-America-specific, service-company-QA → big-tech-TE/SDET account. Recurring pattern in what little is visible: the move is made on DSA/coding + system design preparation over roughly 4–6 months of focused study, after at least one rejection, and it is pitched as "fundamentals + explaining your thought process", not as a portfolio play.

## KQ2. What Google and Microsoft say they evaluate (official pages)

### Takeaway
Google's current careers pages document structured interviews with shared rubrics, role-related open-ended questions, an explicit ban on AI tools during interviews, and optional pre-interview "project work" such as code samples; Microsoft's hiring-tips page says it hires on "skills and shared principles", allows AI in preparation but not in assessments, and may ask for code, a portfolio or work samples. The four-attribute rubric (GCA / role-related knowledge / leadership / Googleyness) and Microsoft's "Core Priorities" could not be located on current official pages.

### Cited Findings
- OFFICIAL (Google, "Our hiring process"): "Typically, you can expect it to take roughly 6-8 weeks"; "Assessments: Before moving into interviews, you may be asked to take the Google Hiring Assessment, designed to measure workstyle skills... You might also be asked to complete additional role-related assessments, like a coding exercise"; "Project work: Depending on the type of role, we may ask candidates to complete a small project prior to their interviews. This could range from prepping a case study to providing writing or code samples"; "Structured interviewing: Every candidate is assessed using clear rubrics. We use the same rubrics for everyone being considered for that role"; "Open-ended questions: We ask role-related, open-ended questions to learn how you solve problems... no brainteasers"; "AI tools are not permitted to be used during your interviews." — [Google Careers: How we hire](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/how-we-hire/)
- OFFICIAL (Google, interview tips): "Cross-reference your resume with the job description... build out a library of examples"; questions are "Tell me about a time when..." or "How would you approach this situation"; "Google loves data... Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]"; "the interviewer will be looking to see the thought process versus the answer itself"; STAR method recommended. — [Google Careers: Interview tips](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/interview-tips/)
- OFFICIAL (Google's own definition of TE vs SET, 2011, James Whittaker, Google Testing Blog): "The SET or Software Engineer in Test is also a developer role except their focus is on testability... TEs are product experts, quality advisers and analyzers of risk." — [Google Testing Blog](https://testing.googleblog.com/2011/02/how-google-tests-software-part-two.html) (dated; the SET title has since become "Software Engineer, Tools and Infrastructure" per the Blind threads below)
- OFFICIAL (Google, live TE listing, "Test Engineer III", Bengaluru, Mid level, fetched 2026-10-13): minimum qualifications "Bachelor's degree or equivalent practical experience. 3 years of experience in coding, developing test methodologies, writing test plans, creating test cases, and debugging. Experience in a programming language such as Python, Java, or C++. Experience working with standard testing tools, automation platforms, and debugging systems." — [Google Careers search "Test Engineer"](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/?q=%22Test%20Engineer%22)

- OFFICIAL (Microsoft hiring tips): Microsoft evaluates "skills and shared principles" and how you "learn, collaborate, and contribute to impact across teams"; looks for "respect, integrity, accountability, and growth mindset"; "2-4 conversations" of up to an hour each; for some roles candidates may be asked to "write code, share a creative portfolio, or provide work samples"; candidates must "represent themselves honestly at every stage"; AI may be used responsibly in preparation but in assessments and interviews candidates should "demonstrate their own skills without outside assistance unless explicitly permitted"; "confirm that you meet the required qualifications outlined in the job posting, before applying". — [Microsoft Careers: Hiring tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips)
- OFFICIAL (Microsoft culture page): four cultural attributes, "Growth mindset, Customer obsessed, Diverse and inclusive, One Microsoft"; "growth isn't just possible—it's expected". — [Microsoft Careers: Culture](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/culture)
- OFFICIAL (Amazon): the interview process page lists "Online Application, Assessments, Phone Screening, Interview Loop" and references "loop interviews", "Bar Raisers" and "Candid Chats". — [amazon.jobs: Interviewing at Amazon](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interviewing-at-amazon)

### Inferences
- Google's official pages now frame the process as rubric-based structured interviews + a workstyle assessment + possible code samples; the "project work / code samples" line is the only official hook for showing a side project before interviews, and it is at Google's request, not the candidate's.
- Both Google and Microsoft now explicitly prohibit AI assistance during interviews, which raises the value of being able to code unaided.

Treat the four-attribute rubric as widely reported but not verified against a live official page in this session.
- Microsoft "Core Priorities" (internal performance framework) was not found on any public Microsoft careers page; the careers FAQ URL 404s.
- Apple and Meta official hiring pages were not fetched.

## KQ3. Résumé / LinkedIn signals sourcers filter on; how "built with Copilot/Claude" reads in 2026

### Takeaway
Google's official résumé guidance is concrete (tailor per job, mirror minimum qualifications, X-Y-Z metrics, be specific about projects and outcomes, no length rule, no cover letter). I found no survey that measures whether "built with Copilot/Claude" on a résumé is read as positive or negative; what exists shows employers worried about AI-enabled cheating in assessments (HackerRank, interviewing.io) while allowing/expecting AI use in preparation and at work (Microsoft, Karat), so the defensible reading is "neutral on a résumé, negative in an interview unless explicitly permitted".

### Cited Findings
- OFFICIAL (Google résumé guidance, verbatim): "start with a blank document and create a resume specifically designed for each job"; "Align your skills and experience with the job description. Tie your work directly to the role qualifications (and don't forget to include data)"; "Ensure you meet the minimum qualifications (MQs) for the role you're applying to, and your resume clearly demonstrates alignment with the MQs"; "Be specific about projects you've worked on or managed. What was the outcome? How did you measure success? When in doubt, lean on the formula, 'accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]'"; "If you've had a leadership role, tell us about it. How big was the team? What was the scope"; "Keep it short. We don't have a length requirement"; "A word on cover letters: we don't require them". — [Google Careers: How we hire](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/how-we-hire/)
- OFFICIAL (Microsoft): "confirm that you meet the required qualifications outlined in the job posting, before applying" and keep profile data that "accurately reflects your skills and experience". — [Microsoft hiring tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips)
- OFFICIAL (Amazon SDET titles, live listings fetched 2026-10-13 via amazon.jobs search API): titles in use are "Software Development Engineer in Test", "Software Dev Engineer - Test", "Software Development Engineer - Test II"; basic qualifications repeatedly include "Experience programming with at least one modern language such as Java, C++, or C# including object-oriented design" and "Experience building test automation frameworks and tools"; a March-2026 SDET II listing (Pune) lists as preferred "Hands-on experience with AI/ML tools for test automation". The title search returned 11 SDET postings; a "Quality Assurance Engineer" search restricted to Canada returned 0. — [amazon.jobs SDET II, Alexa Endpoint Experiences](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/3203393/software-development-engineer-test-ii-alexa-endpoint-experiences); [amazon.jobs search](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/search?base_query=%22Software+Development+Engineer+in+Test%22)
- DATA (interviewing.io interviewer survey, Oct 2025): "33% of FAANG interviewers caught someone cheating"; "81% suspected candidates using AI to cheat"; "Only 11% of FAANG companies use cheating detection software (mostly Meta)"; "58% of FAANG interviewers adjusted algorithmic question types" toward open-ended, conceptual questions; "Only 21% deliberately ask harder questions"; "75% of interviewers believe AI assistance helps weaker candidates pass"; Meta planned AI-assisted onsite interviews in Q3 2025 but kept the algorithmic phone screen; "Zero [FAANG] companies have moved away from algorithmic questions entirely"; 67% of startups report meaningful AI-driven process changes. — [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-is-ai-changing-interview-processes-not-much-and-a-whole-lot)
- DATA (HackerRank Developer Skills Report 2025; 13,732 developers, managers, recruiters, executives and students across 102 countries): "76% of developers report AI makes circumventing assessment systems easier"; "73% feel it's unfair competing against candidates who leverage AI to game tests"; "84% of engineering leaders have raised productivity expectations"; the report says employers "are determining when to allow the use of AI in their hiring process" but gives no percentage of employers allowing it. — [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025)
- DATA (Karat, Nov 4 2025, survey of 400 senior engineering leaders incl. 300+ SVP/CTO/CIO in US, India, China): "human interviews, where candidates are allowed to use AI, produce better business outcomes than automated code tests or AI-prohibited assessments"; "Hiring has overtaken upskilling as the primary AI-readiness strategy"; strong engineers "drive 3x more value than peers" with AI. — [Karat](https://karat.com/resource/2025-ai-workforce-transformation-highlights/)
- DATA (Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025): 84% use or plan to use AI tools (up from 76%); 51% of professional developers use them daily; only 32.7% somewhat/highly trust AI output; 66% cite "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite" as the top frustration; 45.2% say debugging AI-generated code is more time-consuming. No employer/hiring section on AI use was present on the AI page. — [Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- OFFICIAL (Microsoft, on AI in the process): AI tools may be used "responsibly during preparation" but candidates must "demonstrate their own skills without outside assistance unless explicitly permitted". — [Microsoft hiring tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips)
- OFFICIAL (Google): "AI tools are not permitted to be used during your interviews." — [Google How we hire](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/how-we-hire/)
- DATA (interviewing.io, on résumé screening): a separate post, "Are recruiters better than a coin flip at judging resumes? Here's the data.", exists on their blog index (not fetched in this session). — [interviewing.io blog index](https://interviewing.io/blog?page=2)

### Inferences
- The official résumé filter at Google is literally the minimum qualifications of the posting; for a TE posting that means demonstrable "coding, developing test methodologies, writing test plans, creating test cases, and debugging" plus a named language (Python/Java/C++). A résumé titled "Quality Engineer" that does not state the language and automation frameworks will not evidence the MQ.
- Given the cheating statistics and the explicit no-AI rules, listing AI-assisted projects is unlikely to be penalised at screening but will be discounted if the candidate cannot code the same ideas unaided in a live, AI-prohibited interview.

### Gaps
- No published recruiter/sourcer survey ranking "SDET" vs "Software Engineer in Test" vs "Quality Engineer" title keywords was found.
- No 2025–2026 survey measuring hiring-manager sentiment toward "built with Copilot/Claude" on résumés was found (HackerRank 2025 and Karat 2025 address assessment-cheating and AI-allowed interviews, not résumé signalling).
- Google's résumé-tips video (YouTube) and Amazon's résumé advice page were not fetched (the Google blog URL 404s).

## KQ4. Do side projects / open source move the needle?

### Takeaway
No dataset found that isolates side projects or open source as a predictor of big-tech hiring outcomes. The closest evidence (interviewing.io, ~10,000 interviews) says non-brand signals that predicted performance were low-typo résumés, MOOCs, TA roles and concrete, measured accomplishment bullets, i.e., evidence of self-direction and quantified outcomes, which is also what Google's own résumé guidance asks for ("be specific about projects... what was the outcome? How did you measure success?").

### Cited Findings
- DATA: interviewing.io's non-traditional-candidate analysis "provides no specific data on GitHub profiles, open-source contributions, or side projects as predictive signals"; the predictive signals were typos (≤2), MOOCs, TA roles at second-tier schools, and high-signal bullets. — [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-to-spot-great-non-traditional-candidates-when-you-can-t-rely-on-brands)
- OFFICIAL: Google asks candidates to "Be specific about projects you've worked on or managed. What was the outcome? How did you measure success?" and may request "code samples to help us understand how you think and approach problems". — [Google How we hire](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/how-we-hire/)
- OFFICIAL: Microsoft says some roles may be asked to "share a creative portfolio, or provide work samples in other formats". — [Microsoft hiring tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips)
- DATA (HackerRank 2025): the only portfolio-adjacent finding is about employer branding, not candidate evaluation: open-source contributions, community engagement and hackathons are what "most effectively demonstrate company credibility" to developers. — [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025)
- DATA (Amazon SDET II listing, Mar 2026): "Hands-on experience with AI/ML tools for test automation" appears as a preferred qualification, i.e., AI-in-testing experience is now a named screening keyword at Amazon. — [amazon.jobs](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/3203393/software-development-engineer-test-ii-alexa-endpoint-experiences)
- DATA (GitHub Octoverse 2025 landing): "A new developer joins GitHub every second as AI leads TypeScript to #1"; "AI, agents, and typed languages are driving the biggest shifts in software development in more than a decade". No career-signal statistics on the landing page. — [Octoverse 2025](https://octoverse.github.com/)

### Inferences
- What makes a project "count" in the sources that exist is measurement and specificity (an X-measured-by-Y bullet; a code sample that shows how you think), not stars; the report should frame a side project as a source of quantified résumé bullets and an interview story, with the live coding bar as the actual gate.

### Gaps
- No academic or industry study quantifying portfolio/open-source effects on big-tech hiring outcomes was retrievable this session (GitHub hiring-manager surveys, Karat full report, and LinkedIn Economic Graph 2025–26 research pages were either not located or paywalled/"coming soon").

## KQ5. The coding bar for test-focused roles, and a realistic prep plan

### Takeaway
Official sources establish that TE/SDET roles require real coding (Google TE MQ: "3 years of experience in coding"; Amazon SDET: "at least one modern language such as Java, C++, or C#"), that Amazon and Microsoft loops are mostly LeetCode-medium with 3 coding rounds plus system design, and that Google weighs coding above design. The best-documented prep plan found is Tech Interview Handbook's 3 months at 11 h/week over 75 curated problems; the "150–300 problems" figure is community consensus that I could not source this session.

### Cited Findings
- OFFICIAL (Google TE MQ): "3 years of experience in coding... Experience in a programming language such as Python, Java, or C++." — [Google Careers search](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/?q=%22Test%20Engineer%22)
- OFFICIAL (Google, role definition): TE "puts testing first and development second"; SET/SETI "is also a developer role". — [Google Testing Blog, 2011](https://testing.googleblog.com/2011/02/how-google-tests-software-part-two.html)
- ANECDOTE (Blind, 2018, dated): thread "Google SETI": "even TEs are supposed to code to a certain level here, but not by far at the same level as a SETI"; top comment in the same thread begins "I'm a SETI hiring manager and the bar is exactly the same as SWEs. The only difference is that you m[...]" (remainder behind a cookie wall). Thread "Google SETI - is it just a career-killing Test role?" (Mar 2018): "at Google there is SWE, SETI, TE... it was very difficult - Leetcode". — [Blind: Google SETI](https://www.teamblind.com/post/google-seti-xrdb7aqr); [Google SERP, site:teamblind.com](https://www.google.com/search?q=site%3Ateamblind.com+Google+%22test+engineer%22+OR+%22TE%22+interview+coding+difficulty+leetcode+SETI)
- DATA/GUIDE (interviewing.io Google guide): stages are recruiter call → technical phone screen (DS&A, in Google Docs; sometimes two) → onsite 4–6 rounds → team matching; "Google is the only FAANG prioritizing coding over system design"; hiring committee of 4–5 people who did not interview you reviews résumé, referral notes, recruiter notes and all feedback and needs consensus; "The slower you go, the faster you get there"; no recommended problem count or timeline given. — [interviewing.io Google](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/google)
- DATA/GUIDE (interviewing.io Amazon guide): OA via HackerRank with easy-to-medium problems (may be skipped for senior/FAANG-experienced); onsite 4–6 interviews incl. ≥1 behavioral, three coding, one system design, one hiring-manager; "Amazon asks mostly medium-difficulty LeetCode-style coding questions and avoids hard questions", 30–40 minutes per question; a Bar Raiser is in every loop and has veto; "If you have a bad LP round... it's almost always a 'no hire'". No SDET/QAE-specific guidance. — [interviewing.io Amazon](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/amazon)
- DATA/GUIDE (interviewing.io Microsoft guide): recruiter call → phone screen or Codility quiz → onsite (one behavioral, two to three coding, one system design, one domain round) → team matching; coding is "Medium-focused, with some easy questions", heavy on trees, arrays/strings, linked lists, graphs; interviewers recommend C#, Java or Python; process "varies significantly by team". — [interviewing.io Microsoft](https://interviewing.io/guides/hiring-process/microsoft)
- OFFICIAL (Amazon): "Most technical interviews require you to perform coding and system design white boarding exercises." — [amazon.jobs](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/landing_pages/software-development-topics)
- CONSENSUS GUIDE (Tech Interview Handbook, Yangshun, ex-Meta): "3 months (if you can dedicate 11 hours a week) is the recommended period of time for a more holistic preparation"; weeks 1–4 topical study (arrays/strings/hash tables/recursion → sorting/searching/matrices/linked lists/stacks/queues → trees/graphs/heaps/tries → intervals/DP/binary/math), weeks 5–12 the 75 "Grind 75" problems easy→hard; start mock interviews at ~60% completion; DP can be skipped if not relevant. — [Tech Interview Handbook study plan](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/coding-interview-study-plan/)
- DATA (interviewer behaviour change, Oct 2025): 58% of FAANG interviewers now favour open-ended, conceptual variants and 33% probe "why over how" and re-ask with changed parameters. — [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-is-ai-changing-interview-processes-not-much-and-a-whole-lot)

### Inferences
- For a Java engineer who has never done LeetCode: Java is explicitly acceptable in every official MQ found (Google TE: Python/Java/C++; Amazon SDET: Java/C++/C#; Microsoft guide: C#/Java/Python), so the language is not the gap; the gap is DS&A fluency at LeetCode-medium under no-AI conditions, plus a practical system-design round at Amazon/Microsoft.
- A 3-month, ~11 h/week, 75-problem plan is the only sourced plan; doubling it (6 months, ~150 problems) lands inside the unsourced "150–300" community range and matches the 4–6-month prep windows in the one named transition account.

### Gaps
- No interviewing.io (or other) dataset on practice hours vs pass rate was found; their blog index showed no such post.
- The "150–300 problems" and "NeetCode 150" figures are community consensus; no citable source captured this session.
- No official Google statement on a TE-specific coding bar beyond the MQ text; TE-specific interview data (number of coding rounds, difficulty) not found.

## KQ6. "AI-augmented QE": demand for LLM-evaluation / AI-quality roles and what they require

### Takeaway
Evals/quality titles at the frontier labs are research-engineer or safety roles (Anthropic: "Research Engineer, Post-Training Model Evaluations", "Cyber Evaluations Engineer"; OpenAI: "Research Engineer, Frontier Evals & Environments"), Google's AI-quality roles are SWE roles with Python/C++ MQs, and Amazon has started listing "AI/ML tools for test automation" as a preferred SDET qualification. Labour-market data shows AI skills rising fast (agentic-AI postings +280% YoY, 86k+ in 2025, $153k median; "AI literacy" the #1 fastest-growing skill on LinkedIn), but no source gives a count specifically for "LLM evaluation" roles; LinkedIn's guest search shows only loose keyword counts.

## KQ7. Discretion while employed at an Indian IT services firm in Canada

### Takeaway
In Ontario, non-compete clauses in employment contracts have been void since Oct 25 2021 (except executives and business-sale cases), but non-solicit and confidentiality clauses remain enforceable, and the ESA sets no general resignation-notice requirement (the contract may). LinkedIn's own help page says the "Recruiters only" Open-to-Work setting hides the signal from Recruiter users at your current company but "we can't guarantee complete privacy"; the all-members setting adds the public #OpenToWork frame.

### Cited Findings
- OFFICIAL (Ontario ESA guide): "Effective October 25, 2021 employers are prohibited from entering into employment contracts or other agreements with an employee that include a non-compete agreement"; exceptions for a business sale/lease and for "executives" (nine named C-suite titles); "The ESA does not prohibit non-solicit agreements or non-disclosure agreements"; substance over title is what courts examine. — [Ontario.ca ESA guide: non-compete agreements](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/non-compete-agreements)
- OFFICIAL (Ontario ESA guide, termination): the guide sets no general requirement for an employee to give resignation notice; the only statutory resignation notice is in mass-termination situations (one week if employed <2 years, two weeks if ≥2 years). Employer notice/termination pay runs from one week (<1 year) to eight weeks (8+ years). — [Ontario.ca ESA guide: termination of employment](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/termination-employment)
- OFFICIAL (LinkedIn help): visibility options are "All LinkedIn members" (adds the "#OpenToWork photo frame") and "Recruiters only" ("People using LinkedIn Recruiter only"); "we take steps to prevent LinkedIn Recruiter users who work at your company from seeing your shared career interests, but we can't guarantee complete privacy"; the protection keys off "the current company on your LinkedIn profile marked as I am currently working here". — [LinkedIn Help a507508](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508)
- OFFICIAL (Ontario AI-disclosure context): Ontario employers with ≥25 employees must now disclose AI use "in the candidate recruiting or screening process"; 28% of Ontario postings mentioned AI by May 2026. — [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/en-ca/2026/07/08/ontario-job-postings-highlight-the-widespread-use-of-ai-in-hiring-processes/)
- OFFICIAL (Microsoft): candidates must "represent themselves honestly at every stage" and ensure profile data "accurately reflects your skills and experience". — [Microsoft hiring tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips)

### Inferences
- The practical exposure for a TCS-Canada employee is contractual (notice period, non-solicit of clients such as the bank, confidentiality over client code), not a non-compete; a side project must therefore avoid client data, client-specific code and client names.
- "Recruiters only" is the only LinkedIn setting without a public marker, and LinkedIn itself disclaims a guarantee, so the discreet path is Recruiters-only plus keeping the "currently working here" employer accurate.

### Gaps
- TCS Canada's actual standard employment terms (notice length, non-solicit scope, moonlighting/IP clauses) were not retrievable; none of the search paths worked, so this is the candidate's contract to read.
- LinkedIn's help page on muting "share profile changes with your network" was not located (the help ID I tried was a skills article).
